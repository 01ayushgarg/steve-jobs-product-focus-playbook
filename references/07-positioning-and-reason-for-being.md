# 07 · Positioning and a reason for being

> "When I got back here, Apple had forgotten who we were." [BW04, 12 Oct 2004]

He said the same at D5 in 2007: Apple "had to remember who Apple was" [D5-07, 30 May 2007].

## What he said

**The reason-for-being email (29 October 2000).** The Archive's headnote says he often captured his thoughts
by emailing himself notes [MSW-email-reason-for-being, the Archive's headnote]. In one, with the subject line
"Apple's reason for being", he wrote Apple's positioning several times in a row. We paraphrase the drafts
rather than reproduce them; the full email is in *Make Something Wonderful*. In order:

1. Apple as the world's premier company at building high-technology products that ordinary people (*mere
   mortals*) can learn and use.
2. Apple combining state-of-the-art technology with its ease of use, so that users can do more.
3. Apple as "the world's premier bridge builder between mere mortals and the exploding world of high
   technology." [MSW-email-reason-for-being]
4. Apple as the premier company at making that world of technology easy to learn and use, "thereby enabling
   mere mortals to enrich their lives" [MSW-email-reason-for-being].

The same note gives the why: Apple loves the potential of personal computers to enrich the lives of regular
people [MSW-email-reason-for-being, paraphrased].

**Our reading of what changes across the drafts:**
- The **who** stays fixed: ordinary people.
- The **what** sharpens: from building easy products to making technology itself easy.
- The **so what** arrives only in the last draft: what changes in their lives.
- The **claim** is a superlative he had to earn: *premier*.

**The bridge (1996 and 1999).** In 1996 he described the original Mac as "computers for the rest of us"
[MSW-interview-terry-gross-1996]. In January 1999: "It's our job to make complex technology easy to use and fun
to use." [MSW-email-apple-macintosh-fifteen, 24 Jan 1999] He called Apple the *bridge builder* in the same
email [MSW-email-apple-macintosh-fifteen].

**Better, not just different (1997).** At WWDC: "I want to be much better. I don't care about being
different." Then: "We'll have to be different in some ways to be much better." [WWDC97 12:39 to 12:42]
(Machine transcript, made for this repo; checked against a second transcription.)

**Out of the mainstream, you must be far better (1996).** To Terry Gross he said Compaq only had to be 5
percent better than rivals, while Apple had to be "50 percent or 100 percent better, because when you buy
something that is out of the mainstream a little bit, you take a risk" [MSW-interview-terry-gross-1996]. His
diagnosis of Apple then: "their differentiation evaporated." [MSW-interview-terry-gross-1996]

**Keep the goals in order (2003 and 2004).** In 2004: "our primary goal here is to make the world's best PCs
-- not to be the biggest or the richest." Profit came second, so Apple could keep making great products. When
the two goals flipped, he said, "that subtle change made all the difference." [BW04] He told Stanford students
the same story in 2003 [MSW-speech-stanford-2003].

**Product people versus monopoly (2004).** He explained Apple's lost decade as a pattern: good product people
win a monopoly, then the people who expand the business take over from them, and when the monopoly ends the
best product people have gone [BW04]. His fix, on returning: "we had to make it a product company again."
[BW04]

**Two companies, two positions (1996 and 2003).** Pixar was "a pure play to build a feature animation
studio" [MSW-interview-about-pixar, 22 Nov 1996]. Apple, in 2003, was "the most creative of the technology
companies" and Pixar "the most technical of the creative companies" [MSW-speech-stanford-2003].

## How to apply it: five drafts in one sitting (our suggestion)

1. **Fix the who.** Write the customer in plain words. Don't change it between drafts.
2. **Write five drafts without stopping.** Change the what and the so what each time.
3. **Add the so what last.** The final draft must say what changes in the customer's life.
4. **Check the claim.** If you use a superlative, list the evidence. If you can't, drop it.
5. **Size the advantage.** Are you the default or the risky choice? If risky, can you show you're far
   better, not a little?
6. **Check every product against it.** A product that doesn't fit the sentence is a cut candidate
   (chapter 01).
7. **Check the goal order.** Ask the team which comes first, the product or the profit. If they disagree,
   fix that before the sentence.

## A worked scenario (fictional)

A meal-kit startup drafts:

1. Fresh ingredients delivered weekly.
2. The easiest way to cook at home.
3. Dinner without the planning.
4. We plan, shop and prep, so busy parents cook in 20 minutes.
5. We plan, shop and prep, so busy parents get dinner on the table in 20 minutes and eat with their kids.

- **Who:** fixed from draft 4 (busy parents).
- **So what:** *eat with their kids*, added in draft 5.
- **Risk:** they're the challenger to supermarkets, so *a bit easier* isn't enough. Their proof is 20
  minutes against an average 55 (invented).
- **Fit:** their gourmet line for couples doesn't fit. It goes on the say-no list.

## Failure modes and limits (our reading)

- **A sentence for investors, not customers.** If a customer wouldn't recognise themselves, rewrite.
- **Different instead of better.** He wanted *much better* [WWDC97 12:39] and accepted different only as a means.
- **A sentence that fits everything.** If every product fits easily, the sentence may be too vague to cut
  anything.
- **One private email.** The drafts were a note to himself. Treat the five-draft method as ours, inspired by
  that note.

**Checks to run:**
1. Write your reason for being in one sentence: who, what you make easy or possible, and what changes for
   them. Does it hold for every product you sell?
2. Are you the default, or the risky choice? How much better do you need to be?
3. Which goal is first today, the product or the profit? What would your team say?
4. Are your product people still the ones who drive the company?
