# 07 · Positioning and a reason for being

> "Apple had to remember who Apple was because they'd forgotten who Apple was." [D5-07, 30 May 2007] (On 1997.)

## The premier-company email (29 October 2000)

He often emailed notes to himself. One, with the subject line "Apple's reason for being", shows him drafting
Apple's positioning several times in a row [MSW-email-reason-for-being, 29 Oct 2000]. The drafts, in order:

1. "Apple is the world's premier company at building high technology products that are easy to learn and use
   by mere mortals." [MSW-email-reason-for-being]
2. "Apple marries state of the art technology with Apple's legendary ease-of-use to create products that enable
   users to do more" [MSW-email-reason-for-being]
3. "Apple is the world's premier bridge builder between mere mortals and the exploding world of high
   technology." [MSW-email-reason-for-being]
4. "Apple is the premier company in the world at making the exploding world of high technology easy to learn and
   use, thereby enabling mere mortals to enrich their lives using it." [MSW-email-reason-for-being]

The why, in the same note: "Because we are in love with the potential for personal computers to enhance and
enrich the lives of regular people" [MSW-email-reason-for-being].

**Our reading of what changes across the drafts** (phrases from [MSW-email-reason-for-being]):
- The **who** stays fixed: mere mortals, regular people.
- The **what** sharpens: from "products that are easy to learn and use" to "making the exploding world of high
  technology easy to learn and use".
- The **so what** arrives only in the last draft: "thereby enabling mere mortals to enrich their lives".
- The **claim** is a superlative he had to earn: "premier".

**The move:** write your positioning sentence five times in one sitting. Keep the customer fixed. Make the
last draft say what changes in their life.

## Who is Apple? (September 1997)

> "Our customers want to know: Who is Apple, and what is it that we stand for? Where do we fit in this world?"
> [MSW-speech-apple-1997, 23 Sep 1997]

He answered with a value, not a product (chapter 06). **Our reading:** positioning has two layers. The
reason for being says what you do and for whom. The value says why you do it. You need both, and the second
should outlast every product.

## Out of the mainstream, you must be far better (1996)

> "Compaq only has to be 5 percent better than its competitors for everyone to want to buy their computers."
> [MSW-interview-terry-gross-1996]

> "Apple has to be 50 percent or 100 percent better, because when you buy something that is out of the
> mainstream a little bit, you take a risk, and you want a much bigger reward for taking that risk."
> [MSW-interview-terry-gross-1996]

> "That's Apple's problem, is that their differentiation evaporated." [MSW-interview-terry-gross-1996]

**Our reading:** if you're the challenger, size your advantage to the customer's risk in switching. A small
edge is enough only for the default choice.

## Keep the goals in the right order (2003)

> "And so, what happened at Apple was that Apple's goals used to be to make the best personal computers in the
> world. And then the second goal was to make a profit so we could keep on doing number one."
> [MSW-speech-stanford-2003, 29 May 2003]

When the order flipped, "things start to fall apart." [MSW-speech-stanford-2003]

## Know who your customer really is (2010)

Context only, from the D8 liveblog [D8-10, liveblog], which mixes quotes and paraphrase: asked about film and
music, he described asking the music labels who their customer was. As the liveblog reports it, they named
their retailers, and his point was that the real customer was the listener. We don't quote this passage
because the liveblog doesn't clearly mark it as his exact words.

**Use it now:** `templates/05-reason-for-being.md`.

**Checks to run:**
1. Write your reason for being in one sentence: who, what you make easy or possible, and what changes for
   them. Does it hold for every product you sell?
2. Are you the default, or the risky choice? How much better do you need to be?
3. Which goal is first today, the product or the profit? What would your team say?
4. Who is your true customer, as opposed to the channel you sell through?
