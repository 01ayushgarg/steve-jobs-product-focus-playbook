# 08 · Decisions and mistakes

> the way." [WWDC97 54:48]

## Match the decision speed to the cost of being wrong (2003)

He ran Apple and Pixar at the same time and described them as opposite ends of a spectrum
[MSW-speech-stanford-2003, 29 May 2003]:

> "Apple is a company that has new products every few weeks. It's a company where you make ten important
> decisions a day, but if some of them are wrong, most of them are not terribly hard to correct a few months
> down the road." [MSW-speech-stanford-2003]

> "As CEO, you make a few important decisions a quarter—maybe three—but they are very hard to change if you
> decide you want to change them." [MSW-speech-stanford-2003] (Pixar, with one film a year at best.)

**The move:** sort your decisions into the two kinds. Make the reversible ones fast and many. Spend your time
on the few you can't undo.

## Don't be afraid to fail (1996)

> "One of the things I always tried to coach myself on was not being afraid to fail."
> [MSW-interview-terry-gross-1996, 1996]

> "I think that's a big mistake, because you never achieve what you want without falling on your face a few
> times in the process of getting there." [MSW-interview-terry-gross-1996] (On people who become "very
> protective" after a failure.)

> "I've tried to not be afraid to fail, and, matter of fact, I've failed quite a bit since leaving Apple."
> [MSW-interview-terry-gross-1996]

## Ask to be challenged, and change your mind (1995 and 1997)

To his head of software, in August 1997: "Please continue to challenge me. It's the way we get to the right
decisions, and I enjoy it too." [MSW-email-avie, 25 Aug 1997]

In 1995, after Intel's Andy Grove pushed back on his demand that Intel pay for Pixar's graphics advice: "I
have changed my position 180 degrees" [MSW-email-andy-grove, 1 Oct 1995].

On management by values: if everyone agrees on the destination, they can argue about the route. "So if I can
get there in an hour [flying], I'll ditch my idea about the train." [MSW-speech-stanford-2003]

**Our reading:** agree on the destination first, then let the best route win, even if it isn't yours. Most
arguments that go nowhere are really about the destination.

## Say no, and say it once

From chapter 01: "Our decision to end Newton development was not taken lightly, and is unlikely to be

## Grade yourself, in writing

At the end of 1997 he graded Pixar's leadership against the milestones he'd written in October 1996: four A's,
one B and two F's, including "Ship Two Strong Interactive Products" graded "F (but A for getting out)"
[MSW-email-pixar-grades, 21 Dec 1997]. Five days later he sent the 1998 list:

> "The purpose of this list is to remind us of the" ... "from time to time, and to help prod us into taking
> corrective action when we get off track." [MSW-email-pixar-forest, 26 Dec 1997]

> "Please feel free to shoot full of holes with your comments." [MSW-email-pixar-forest]

**The move:** write down a short list of milestones, grade it honestly at the end of the year, and give
credit for stopping something that wasn't working.

  30 May 2007]
