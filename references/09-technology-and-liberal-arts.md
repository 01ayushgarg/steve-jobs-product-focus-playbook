# 09 · Technology and the liberal arts

> "I think our major contribution was in bringing a liberal arts point of view to the use of computers."
> [MSW-interview-terry-gross-1996, 1996]

## What he meant by it (1996)

> "Our goal was to bring a liberal arts perspective and a liberal arts audience to what had traditionally been a
> very geeky technology and a very geeky audience." [MSW-interview-terry-gross-1996]

The examples were concrete: fonts and typography, and graphics "so that they could see beautiful photographs,
or pictures, or artwork" [MSW-interview-terry-gross-1996]. And the belief underneath: "science and computer
science is a liberal art." [MSW-interview-terry-gross-1996]

In 1999 he wrote that the Mac's "infusion of graphics and typography placed the Macintosh at the intersection
of computer science and liberal arts." [MSW-email-apple-macintosh-fifteen, 24 Jan 1999]

## Where it came from: one calligraphy class (2005)

> "I learned about serif and sans serif typefaces, about varying the amount of space between different letter
> combinations, about what makes great typography great." [STAN05]

> "None of this had even a hope of any practical application in my life. But 10 years later, when we were
> designing the first Macintosh computer, it all came back to me." [STAN05]

> "It was the first computer with beautiful typography." [STAN05]

His 1996 version adds the next step: the same insight "also led to the LaserWriter printer, so that one could
print these letterforms with the quality they deserved." [MSW-speech-palo-alto, Jun 1996]

## His strength, in his words (1990s)

> "My strength probably is that I've always viewed technology from a liberal arts perspective, from a human
> culture perspective." [MSW-interview-company-of-giants, 1990s]

> "All of the desktop publishing stuff on the Mac comes from books: the typography, that rich feel that nobody
> in computers knew anything about." [MSW-interview-company-of-giants]

## Artists and engineers are the same kind of person (1995)

> "I actually think there's actually very little distinction between an artist and a scientist or engineer of
> the highest calibre." [SI95]

> "The things I'm most proud about at Apple is where the technical and the humanistic came together, as it did
> in publishing for example." [SI95]

On the early Mac team: "Almost all of them were musicians. Alot of them were poets on the side." [SI95]

## Pixar: two cultures in one building (1996 and 2003)

> "I learned this at Pixar: technology companies and content companies have absolutely no understanding of each
> other." [MSW-speech-stanford-2003, 29 May 2003]

> "The creative process is as disciplined as any engineering process I've ever seen in my life."
> [MSW-speech-stanford-2003]

> "I think Pixar is the only place in the world that can hire the best from both of these areas."
> [MSW-interview-about-pixar, 22 Nov 1996]

And the limit of technology: "And no amount of technology can turn a bad story into a good story."
[MSW-interview-about-pixar]

## Creativity is connecting things (1996)

> "Creativity equals connecting previously unrelated experiences and insights that others don't see."
> [MSW-speech-palo-alto, Jun 1996]

> "You have to have them to connect them." [MSW-speech-palo-alto]

**The move:** put people who know the humanities next to people who know the technology, on the same team, with
the same goal. And feed your own range: read, look and listen outside your field.

## Bring it into business (1984)

> "So if we can again inject that liberal-arts spirit into this very serious realm of business, I think it would
> be a worthwhile contribution." [MSW-on-the-macintosh, 1984]

His example was the memo: with fonts and pictures, "you're seeing five-page memos get compressed down to a
one-page memo because there's a picture to express the key concept." [MSW-on-the-macintosh]

**Our reading:** the liberal-arts test for a product is whether it helps people express something, not only
compute something. For a team, it's whether anyone in the room is judging the work as a reader, a viewer or a
listener would.

**Use it now:** `templates/02-customer-experience-first-spec.md` (the "how it should feel" section).

**Checks to run:**
1. Who on your team judges the product the way a writer, designer or musician would?
2. Where does your product help people express something, not just process something?
3. What did you learn outside your field in the last year that could change the product?
4. Is your "creative" side as disciplined as your engineering side? Is your engineering side as curious?
