# 03 · Simplicity and taste

> "[At Apple] we're just getting simpler and simpler and simpler. Very, very simple."
> [MSW-interview-michael-moritz-1984, May 1984]

## Taste is stubbornness, not genius

> "I don't think my taste in aesthetics is that much different than a lot of other people's. The difference is
> that I just get to be really stubborn about making things as good as we all know they can be."
> [MSW-interview-michael-moritz-1984]

> "Your aesthetics get better as you make mistakes." [MSW-interview-michael-moritz-1984]

> "And I think, in the same way, good aesthetics result from just your eye." [MSW-interview-michael-moritz-1984]
> (He compares it to a singer whose voice is good because her ear is good.)

What fed his eye in 1984: "Just a bunch of little things: wine labels, paintings in galleries."
[MSW-interview-michael-moritz-1984] And for the Mac: "I went around and looked at Cuisinarts when we were
designing Mac." [MSW-interview-michael-moritz-1984]

**The move:** taste is a habit you can train. Look at well-made things outside your field, every week, and
refuse to ship what you already know could be better.

## Great costs time, not money

> "But the real big thing is: if you're going to make something, it doesn't take any more energy—and rarely does
> it take more money—to make it really great. All it takes is a little more time."
> [MSW-interview-michael-moritz-1984]

To designers in 1983: "And it doesn't cost any more money to make them look great." [MSW-speech-aspen-1983,
15 Jun 1983] And: "We have a chance to make these things beautiful, and we have a chance to communicate
something through the design of the objects themselves." [MSW-speech-aspen-1983]

## Simplicity in code: the line you never wrote (May 1997)

`[WWDC97]` is a machine transcript of the archive.org recording, made for this repo; check wording against the
video.

> "The way you get programmer productivity is by eliminating lines of code you have to write." [WWDC97 41:38]

> "The line of code that's the fastest to write that never breaks, that doesn't need maintenance, is the line you
> never had to write." [WWDC97 41:45]

> "It's all about managing complexity." [WWDC97 25:23]

**Our reading:** the same applies to features, settings and steps. The fastest feature to support is the one
you didn't add.

## Simplicity for the user: the bridge (1999)

> "It's our job to make complex technology easy to use and fun to use."
> [MSW-email-apple-macintosh-fifteen, 24 Jan 1999]

> "The computer world shows no signs of getting simpler as we enter the coming century."
> [MSW-email-apple-macintosh-fifteen]

And in 2007, on the iPhone: "making complex technology easy to use and self-discoverable."
[MSW-speech-apple-2007]

## Editing: what's on, and what's not

> "I think the art of it is balancing what's on there and what's not on there, is the editing function."
> [D5-07, 30 May 2007] (On what the pocket device of the future should do.)

At Pixar, the headquarters building went the way a film does: "First, a treatment; throw it away. Then another;
throw it away." Then "zillions and zillions of iterations, model after model after model, detail after
detail." [MSW-speech-pixar-2000, Nov 2000]

## Fit and finish means how it works, not just how it looks

On the NeXT computer in 1995: "Second of all, the machine had a fit and finish that you don't find today."
Then: "I don't just mean in packaging; I mean in terms of operation. Simple things to complex things." [SI95]
His example was soft power: you press a button, and the computer "figures out what it needs to do to shut down
gracefully and then turns itself off." [SI95]

## Design matters more as prices fall

> "We believe that as the price points come down, as the consumer market blossoms, design and fashion become
> even more important." [MSW-speech-macworld-1998, Jul 1998]

On smaller products, in 1984: "And when you make things smaller, you have the ability to make them more
precisely." [MSW-interview-michael-moritz-1984]

## His own caveat: best is the enemy of better

> "In certain cases, my weaknesses are that I'm too idealistic." [MSW-interview-company-of-giants, 1990s]

> "I'm not always wise enough to know when to go for the best and when to just go for better."
> [MSW-interview-company-of-giants]

> "Balancing the ideal and the practical is something I still must pay attention to."
> [MSW-interview-company-of-giants]

**Our reading:** be stubborn about the few things the customer touches most. Go for "better" on the rest, and
ship.
