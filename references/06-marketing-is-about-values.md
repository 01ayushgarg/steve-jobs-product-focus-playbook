# 06 · Marketing is about values

> "To me, marketing is about values." [MSW-speech-apple-1997, 23 Sep 1997]

The Archive's headnote to this speech says he gave it to a small group of Apple employees on 23 September 1997,
before showing them the first *Think Different* ad [MSW-speech-apple-1997, the Archive's headnote]. The talk
is the clearest statement in his own words of how he thought about marketing. This chapter adds what he said
about the campaign in 2004, about advertising in 1997 and 2003, and about tone in 2007.

## What he said

**One message in a noisy world (1997).** He called it *a very noisy world* in which no company gets people to
remember much about it, so "we have to be really clear on what we want them to know about us."
[MSW-speech-apple-1997]

**Not speeds and feeds (1997).** The way to do that, he said, is "not to talk about speeds and feeds"
[MSW-speech-apple-1997], nor about chip speeds, nor about why Apple beat Windows. His examples: the dairy
industry's milk campaign, which barely mentioned the product, and Nike. "Nike sells a commodity!" Yet Nike's
ads "honor great athletes, and they honor great athletics." [MSW-speech-apple-1997]

**Who are we? (1997).** The question the team began with: "Who is Apple, and what is it that we stand for?
Where do we fit in this world?" [MSW-speech-apple-1997] His answer was that Apple wasn't about making boxes
for people to get their jobs done, though it did that well. Its core value was a belief that "people with
passion can change the world for the better." [MSW-speech-apple-1997] Products and distribution change, he
said: "But values and core values: those things shouldn't change." [MSW-speech-apple-1997]

**The campaign was for the company too (2004).** Looking back, he said *Think Different* was partly for
customers, "but it was even more for Apple itself." [BW04, 12 Oct 2004] His reasoning: you can tell a lot
about people from their heroes, and the ad was meant to remind Apple "of who our heroes are and who we are."
[BW04]

**When not to advertise (May 1997).** Four months before the campaign, as an adviser, he argued Apple
shouldn't be on television that year, because a big campaign would look like Apple spending money "to
convince you that everything's okay." [WWDC97 47:23] What Apple needed instead was journalists reporting that
it was back [WWDC97 48:05], and profits: "the high order bit of any marketing campaign is profitability."
[WWDC97 49:27] (Machine transcript, made for this repo; checked against a second transcription.)

**Not every innovation gets an ad (2003).** "If we [Apple] come up with a dozen innovations in a year, we can
maybe advertise four or five of them." [MSW-speech-stanford-2003, 29 May 2003] The rest, he said, had to be
shown where people buy, which is why Apple opened its own stores (chapter 12).

**Tone (2007).** On the Mac and PC ads: "The art of those commercials is not to be mean, but it's actually for
the guys to like each other." [D5-07, 30 May 2007]

**Values can rot (1995).** "What ruined Apple wasn't growth. What ruined Apple was values." [SI95] And in 1998:
"Brands take decades to build." [MSW-speech-macworld-1998]

## How to apply it: the values message (our suggestion)

1. **Write the one thing.** If people remember one thing about you, what should it be? One sentence.
2. **Separate the box from the value.** Write what you make. Then write what you're about, starting *We
   believe that...*.
3. **Pick who you honour.** Nike honoured athletes. Name the customers or people your message celebrates.
   It shouldn't be you.
4. **Strip the specs.** Move every number and comparison out of the headline. Keep them for the point of
   sale.
5. **Check it against the product.** List three product decisions that prove the value. If you can't, the
   message isn't true yet.
6. **Check the timing.** Is the company healthy enough that the campaign won't be undone by bad news next
   quarter? If not, earn the story first.
7. **Test it inside.** Read it to the team. If it doesn't remind them who they are, it won't convince
   anyone outside.

## A worked scenario (fictional)

A bookkeeping app for freelancers leads its homepage with *Sync 12,000 banks. 99.9% uptime.*

- **One thing:** you'll never dread tax season again.
- **Value:** we believe independent people deserve the back office of a big company.
- **Who we honour:** freelancers who built a living on their own.
- **Specs:** bank count and uptime move to the pricing page.
- **Proof:** auto-categorised receipts, a quarterly tax estimate, and a human accountant on call.
- **Timing:** churn is high this quarter (invented). They fix onboarding first and launch the campaign in
  three months.

## Failure modes and limits (our reading)

- **A value the product contradicts.** A message the product doesn't live up to is worse than no message.
- **Values as decoration.** If the value would change when the product line changes, it's a feature.
- **Attacking competitors.** He said not to talk about why Apple was better than Windows. Comparison ads
  invite a spec fight.
- **Campaigns to cover bad news.** His 1997 advice was the opposite: fix the business, then advertise.
- **One speech, one company.** Most of this comes from a single 1997 talk about a famous brand. A small
  company may need specs earlier in the funnel. Treat this as a test of the headline, not a ban on facts.

**Checks to run:**
1. In one sentence: what do you want customers to know about you? Would your team write the same sentence?
2. Does your marketing talk about the customer, or about your specs?
3. Which of your values would you keep if every product changed?
4. Who are your heroes, and does your message show it?
