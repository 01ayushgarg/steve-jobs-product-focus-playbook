# 06 · Marketing is about values

> "To me, marketing is about values." [MSW-speech-apple-1997, 23 Sep 1997]

On 23 September 1997 he showed the first "Think Different" ad to a small group of Apple employees. The talk
before it is the clearest statement of how he thought about marketing. All quotes in the first three sections
are from that talk [MSW-speech-apple-1997].

## A noisy world: you get one message

> "This is a very complicated world. It's a very noisy world, and we're not gonna get a chance to get people to
> remember much about us. No company is." [MSW-speech-apple-1997]

> "And so we have to be really clear on what we want them to know about us." [MSW-speech-apple-1997]

**The move:** decide the one thing you want people to remember before you write a single ad, page or post. If
you can't say it in a sentence, you're not ready to market.

## Not speeds and feeds

> "The way to do that is not to talk about speeds and feeds. It's not to talk about MIPS and megahertz. It's not
> to talk about why we are better than Windows." [MSW-speech-apple-1997]

His examples were the dairy industry's milk campaign, which "doesn't even talk about the product" [MSW-speech-apple-1997], and Nike:

> "Remember: Nike sells a commodity! They sell shoes! And yet when you think of Nike, you feel something
> different than a shoe company." [MSW-speech-apple-1997]

> "What does Nike do in their advertising? They honor great athletes, and they honor great athletics. That's
> who they are." [MSW-speech-apple-1997]

> "Apple spends a fortune on advertising. You'd never know it. You'd never know it." [MSW-speech-apple-1997]

## Say what you stand for, and keep it

> "Our customers want to know: Who is Apple, and what is it that we stand for? Where do we fit in this world?"
> [MSW-speech-apple-1997] (The question the team asked when they started the campaign.)

> "And what we're about isn't making boxes for people to get their jobs done, although we do that well."
> [MSW-speech-apple-1997]

> "Apple, at the core—its core value—is that we believe that people with passion can change the world for the
> better." [MSW-speech-apple-1997]

> "But values and core values: those things shouldn't change." [MSW-speech-apple-1997] (Products, distribution
> and manufacturing, he said, were all "totally different" from a decade before.)

> "But even a great brand needs investment and caring if it's going to retain its relevance and vitality."
> [MSW-speech-apple-1997]

## The medium is part of the message (May 1997)

Four months earlier, as an adviser, he argued against Apple advertising on TV that year. `[WWDC97]` is a
machine transcript of the archive.org recording, made for this repo; check wording against the video.

> "And I personally believe that Apple should not be on television at all this year." [WWDC97 47:15]

> "Because what it means is that Apple is trying to spend a lot of money to convince you that everything's
> okay." [WWDC97 47:23]

> "It needs for the journalists to be saying Apple's back on page one." [WWDC97 48:05]

> "And I also believe very strongly that the high order bit of any marketing campaign is profitability."
> [WWDC97 49:27]

His reasoning: a costly campaign during a losing quarter undoes itself. "If we lose money in that quarter, any
positive momentum that we've created is completely erased." [WWDC97 49:41]

## Not every innovation gets an ad (2003)

> "If we [Apple] come up with a dozen innovations in a year, we can maybe advertise four or five of them."
> [MSW-speech-stanford-2003, 29 May 2003]

> "You have to communicate those with the customer at the point of sale." [MSW-speech-stanford-2003] (This was
> his explanation for opening Apple's own stores.)

The stores followed the same logic of going to the customer: "We decided we had to ambush them."
[MSW-speech-stanford-2003] That meant paying for high-traffic locations, so people "just had to take the risk
of walking ten feet" [MSW-speech-stanford-2003].

## Tone: confident, not mean (2007)

> "The art of those commercials is not to be mean, but it's actually for the guys to like each other." [D5-07,
> 30 May 2007] (On Apple's Mac and PC ads.)

## Values can rot

> "What ruined Apple wasn't growth. What ruined Apple was values." [SI95] (His 1995 view of Apple after he
> left.)

> "Brands take decades to build." [MSW-speech-macworld-1998, Jul 1998]

**Our reading:** a values message only works if the company actually acts on the value. "Think Different"
honoured people who changed things, and the products were meant to be tools for them. A message your product
contradicts is worse than no message.

**Checks to run:**
1. In one sentence: what do you want customers to know about you? Would your team write the same sentence?
2. Does your marketing talk about the customer and what they care about, or about your specs?
3. Which of your values would you keep if every product changed? Which are really just features?
4. Is any campaign trying to convince people that everything is OK, when the product or the numbers should do that?
