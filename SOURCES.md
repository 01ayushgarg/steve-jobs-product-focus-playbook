# Sources

Every quote in this repo is from one of the sources below. Before publishing, each quote was checked by a
private script against our saved plain-text copy of the source it cites, allowing only quote-mark, dash and
whitespace differences; *...* joins exact fragments. The saved copies aren't redistributed (they're
copyrighted), so the script isn't shipped. You can check any quote against the links below.

## Labelling rules

- **His words** are in quotation marks with a citation. Words of the Archive or a publisher are labelled as
  theirs. Titles, slogans and prompts in templates (such as *Think Different* or a question to ask a
  candidate) may be in quotation marks without a citation; they aren't quotes of him.
- **Paraphrase** is plain text with a citation. Much of v2 paraphrases rather than quotes, to keep each short
  work well under a tenth quoted.
- **Our reading**, **Our suggestion** and **The move:** mark our own interpretation, steps, thresholds and
  applications. Every number in a worked example is invented and labelled.
- **`[sic]`** marks a transcription error in `[WWDC97]`, with the likely word where it's clear (for example
  *Laser Rider, [sic: LaserWriter]*), and one typo in the D8 liveblog (*they [sic: the] way*).
- **...** (three dots) joins exact fragments where we've cut words. In `[WWDC97]` quotes it also marks a break between
  transcript segments. In *Make Something Wonderful*, the Archive's own cuts (marked [...] or ✂ in the book)
  are never bridged silently.
- **The Archive's words** (headnotes, editor's notes, timeline) are labelled as such and never presented as
  his.
- **Dates** are given for every source and, for the book, every section.
- **Fictional examples** are labelled as fictional.

## Conflicts and caveats

- **Fifteen or seventeen?** At Macworld in July 1998 he said Apple had "fifteen product platforms" when he
  returned [MSW-speech-macworld-1998]. The Archive's editor's note says he "slashed the company's product
  offerings from seventeen to four" [MSW-part-iii-editors-note]. In September 1997 he described cutting "70
  percent of the stuff on the product road map" [MSW-speech-apple-1997]. The playbook uses his own figures.
- **WWDC 1997 was before he ran Apple.** In May 1997 he was an adviser and said so ("But I don't make these
  decisions." [WWDC97 50:10]). The product cut and *Think Different* came after he became interim CEO, which
  the Archive dates to September 1997 [MSW-key-events].
- **WWDC97 is a machine transcript.** No official Apple transcript was found. In v2 its role was reduced
  (from 65 quotes in v1 to 32, and from about 100 mentions to about 60): where another first-party source says the same thing, that source
  is cited instead. The remaining quoted lines matched a second transcription with a larger model. Lines that
  didn't match were dropped or paraphrased, for example the figure of *10% or 20% or 30%* of technology to
  invent (chapter 04) and the remark before *I cannot imagine being successful trying to manage three*
  (chapter 01), where both transcriptions give a sentence that doesn't fit its context. Lines with garbled audio (for
  example the sentence just before *Focusing is about saying no* [WWDC97 06:02]) were left out rather than repaired. The
  YouTube auto-captions of the same session were also collected but not used, because they are partial and
  the archive.org recording is more complete.
- **SI95 is edited.** The Smithsonian page presents excerpts of a longer interview, edited by a transcript
  editor. Quotes are exact to that text.
- **Two versions of the Stanford address.** Stanford's text [STAN05] and the Archive's version
  (`MSW-speech-stanford-commencement-2005`) differ in small ways (for example *backward* and *backwards*).
  The playbook quotes Stanford's text.
- **Two versions of the Alan Kay line.** At D5 he quoted Kay as "People that love software want to build their
  own hardware." [D5-07] At the iPhone launch: "People who are really serious about software should make their
  own hardware." [MSW-speech-macworld-2007] Both are his recollections of Kay's words. Both are shown in
  chapter 04.
- **The whole widget.** At WWDC in 1997 he called end-to-end control Apple's "greatest strength if managed
  right" [WWDC97 19:27]. In 1995 he called making the whole widget a mistake at NeXT [SI95]. Chapter 04 shows
  both.
- **D5 transcripts.** The AllThingsD transcript [D5-07] and the Archive's excerpt
  (`MSW-interview-all-things-d-2007`) differ slightly in punctuation. The playbook quotes the AllThingsD text.
- **Edited interviews.** SI95, WIRED96, BW04 and FORTUNE08 are edited by their publishers. Quotes are exact to
  the published text, including the publishers' bracketed insertions.
- **Fortune page 1.** The archived copy of page 1 (on the birth of the iPhone) has no text, so it isn't used.
- **MIT92 is a caption transcript.** It contains [INAUDIBLE] marks and run-on punctuation. Quotes are exact to
  it.
- **Process versus content.** An idea sometimes attributed to his 1992 MIT talk (confusing process with
  content) does not appear in the MIT92 caption transcript, so it isn't quoted or attributed.
- **One More Thing (1994).** The Archive dates this passage to 1994 but its venue isn't in our sources.

## What we didn't use, and why

- **Walter Isaacson's 2011 biography, and other biographies and profiles:** secondary sources. Not used.
- **Quote-aggregator sites and *Jobs' rules* listicles:** not used.
- **Famous lines not in these sources:** phrases often attributed to him, such as *real artists ship*,
  *insanely great* (as his own phrase) and *a dent in the universe*, don't appear in the sources above, so
  they aren't quoted. (Saying no to *1,000 things* does appear, in BW04, and is now quoted from there.)
- **The Archive's editorial text** (preface, introduction, editor's notes, key events, credits): the Archive's
  words, not his. Used only for dates and one labelled figure.
- **Lines spoken by others** at D5 and in the book's interviews and email exchanges (Gates, Mossberg, Swisher,
  interviewers, Andy Grove, Avie Tevanian, an Intel engineer, a customer): not quoted as his. Bill Gates's
  praise of his taste at D5 is Gates speaking, so it isn't used.
- **The D8 liveblog, beyond context:** paraphrase and reader comments are not quoted.
- **Anything about politics, his private life, his health beyond the Stanford address, or disputes:** out of
  scope. This includes the political and policy passages in SI95, WIRED96 and MIT92, and parts of the D8
  liveblog.
- **Unofficial transcripts:** fan-made transcripts of talks (for example of the 1995 *lost interview*) were
  not used.
